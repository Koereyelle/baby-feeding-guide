Nutritionist Tips

Guidance for 6–12 months

🍼 Starting solids safely

Breast milk or formula first. Solids are supplemental at 6 months — offer the breast or bottle first, then solids 30–60 minutes later.
One new food at a time. Wait 3–5 days before introducing another new ingredient to isolate any reaction.
Start with 1–2 teaspoons. Gradually increase to 2–3 tablespoons per meal by month 2. Rejection is normal — it can take 10–15 exposures.
No honey before age 1. It can contain botulism spores that are dangerous to infants.

🥦 Nutrition priorities

Iron is #1. Breast milk is low in iron after 6 months. Lentils, fortified oats, peas, and spinach are great sources. Pair with vitamin C foods (mango, pear) to boost absorption.
Healthy fats matter. Avocado is a powerhouse — fat is critical for brain development.
Vitamin D supplement. If exclusively breastfed, your pediatrician will likely recommend 400 IU/day.

😋 Texture progression by month

6–7 months: Smooth, thin purées. No lumps.
7–9 months: Mashed foods with soft lumps. Fork-mashed banana, slightly chunky lentil soup.
9–12 months: Soft finger foods, minced proteins, soft-cooked vegetables cut small.
12 months+: Most family foods with minimal adaptation. Continue to avoid choking hazards.

🚫 Avoid before age 1

Honey · Whole cow's milk as a drink · Added salt or sugar · Whole grapes · Whole nuts · Hard raw vegetables · Large pieces of any food
